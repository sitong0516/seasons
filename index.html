Four Seasons of My Teenage Years

Growing up in Boston, Massachusetts

Looking Back at Boston

During my teenage years in Boston, Massachusetts, each season felt different. The weather shaped my plans, from shoveling snow to playing golf and going boating. These memories describe how the seasons felt to me, with approximate temperatures rather than exact weather records.

Spring: A Slow Warm-Up

Spring in Boston always seemed to take its time. I remember chilly mornings and afternoons that felt closer to 50 or 60 degrees Fahrenheit. Rain came regularly, usually as light showers that left sidewalks wet and muddy patches on the grass. I kept a jacket nearby because a sunny morning could turn into a cold afternoon.

Spring: Getting Outside Again

As a teenager, I looked forward to spending more time outside after winter. Melting snow sometimes left puddles along the streets, and rainy stretches could last for several days. I enjoyed seeing leaves return and taking walks when the weather cleared. Even a short warm afternoon felt exciting after months of heavy coats.

Summer: Heat on the Water

Some summer days in Boston felt surprisingly hot, with temperatures in the 80s and occasionally around 90 degrees Fahrenheit. I remember going boating and feeling the strong sunlight on my face. The breeze over the water sometimes felt cool, but the direct sun still made me sweaty and uncomfortable. I learned to appreciate cold water, sunscreen, and any shade I could find.

Summer: A Golf Outing in Heavy Rain

One summer golf outing ended with a sudden downpour. Rain fell so heavily that my clothes were completely soaked before I could get under cover. Water quickly collected in puddles, and we had to stop playing. Summer rain was not always steady, but that short, intense storm was enough to ruin our plans and become a memory I still laugh about.

Autumn: Cooler Air and Colorful Leaves

Autumn was one of the most comfortable seasons during my teenage years. Early fall afternoons often felt like the 60s, while later days could feel closer to the 40s or 50s. The air became cooler, and the leaves changed to red, orange, and yellow. I liked wearing a sweatshirt and walking outside without the exhausting summer heat.

Autumn: Rain and Shorter Days

Fall also brought occasional rainy days and chilly winds. Most rain I remember was light or moderate, although a heavier shower could leave wet leaves stuck to the sidewalks. It became dark earlier, so there was less time to spend outside after school. By late autumn, cold mornings reminded me that snow and winter chores would soon return.

Winter: Deep Snow in Boston

Winter is the season I remember most clearly because of Boston's heavy snowstorms. Temperatures often felt below freezing, especially when the wind hit my face. During the biggest storms I remember, snow piled up well over a foot and made parked cars look buried. The streets became quiet, and familiar places looked completely different under thick snow.

Winter: Shoveling Before We Could Drive

During snowy stretches, shoveling felt like an everyday routine. We had to clear the driveway and the snow around the car, or we could not drive out. Sometimes more snow fell after we had finished, which meant doing the work again. Although I enjoyed how beautiful the snow looked, those tiring mornings taught me that a Boston winter also meant responsibility.

Four seasons, one city, and memories of growing up.
